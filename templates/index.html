Jacobi
Iteration

An iterative algorithm for solving systems of linear equations \( Ax = b \) by decomposing \( A \) into its diagonal and remainder components.

Theory Examples Calculator
01 — Mathematical Discussion

Theory & Formulation

Core Idea

Given a linear system \( Ax = b \) with \( A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n} \), we decompose \( A \) as:

\[ A = D + L + U \]

where \( D \) is the diagonal matrix, \( L \) is the strictly lower-triangular part, and \( U \) is the strictly upper-triangular part.

The Jacobi iteration formula isolates the diagonal:

\[ x_i^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{a_{ii}} \left( b_i - \sum_{j \neq i} a_{ij} x_j^{(k)} \right), \quad i = 1, 2, \ldots, n \]

In matrix form:

\[ x^{(k+1)} = D^{-1}(b - (L+U)x^{(k)}) = D^{-1}b - D^{-1}(L+U)x^{(k)} \]

Algorithm Steps

  1. Choose an initial guess \( x^{(0)} \) (commonly the zero vector).
  2. At each iteration \( k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots \), compute all new components simultaneously using only values from the previous iteration: \[ x_i^{(k+1)} = \frac{b_i - \sum_{j=1, j\neq i}^{n} a_{ij} x_j^{(k)}}{a_{ii}} \]
  3. Check convergence: stop when \[ \max_i \left| x_i^{(k+1)} - x_i^{(k)} \right| < \varepsilon \] for a chosen tolerance \( \varepsilon \).
  4. Repeat until convergence or maximum iterations reached.

Convergence Condition

The Jacobi method is guaranteed to converge if \( A \) is strictly diagonally dominant:

\[ |a_{ii}| > \sum_{j \neq i} |a_{ij}|, \quad \forall\, i \]

Equivalently, convergence occurs when the spectral radius of the iteration matrix \( T_J = -D^{-1}(L+U) \) satisfies \( \rho(T_J) < 1 \).

Key Properties

  • Parallel All \( x_i^{(k+1)} \) computed independently — naturally parallelizable.
  • Memory Requires storing two full vectors simultaneously.
  • Cost Each iteration costs \( O(n^2) \) for dense \( A \).
  • vs Gauss-Seidel Uses old values throughout; typically converges slower but is simpler to parallelize.
02 — Worked Examples

Step-by-Step Solutions

Example 1 3×3 Diagonally Dominant System

Problem: Solve the system:

\[ \begin{cases} 10x_1 - x_2 + 2x_3 = 6 \\ -x_1 + 11x_2 - x_3 = 25 \\ 2x_1 - x_2 + 10x_3 = -11 \end{cases} \] \[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & -1 & 2 \\ -1 & 11 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 10 \end{bmatrix}, \quad b = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 25 \\ -11 \end{bmatrix} \]

Exact solution: \( x_1 = 1,\; x_2 = 2,\; x_3 = -1 \)

Step 1 — Check Diagonal Dominance

Row 1: \( |10| > |-1| + |2| = 3 \) ✓

Row 2: \( |11| > |-1| + |-1| = 2 \) ✓

Row 3: \( |10| > |2| + |-1| = 3 \) ✓

The system is strictly diagonally dominant → convergence guaranteed.

Step 2 — Jacobi Formulas
\[ x_1^{(k+1)} = \frac{6 + x_2^{(k)} - 2x_3^{(k)}}{10} \] \[ x_2^{(k+1)} = \frac{25 + x_1^{(k)} + x_3^{(k)}}{11} \] \[ x_3^{(k+1)} = \frac{-11 - 2x_1^{(k)} + x_2^{(k)}}{10} \]

Start with \( x^{(0)} = [0, 0, 0]^T \)

Example 2 4×4 System – Heat Conduction Analogy

Problem: Solve the 4×4 system arising from a simple finite-difference heat equation:

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -1 & 0 & 0\\ -1 & 4 & -1 & 0\\ 0 & -1 & 4 & -1\\ 0 & 0 & -1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}, \quad b = \begin{bmatrix} 15 \\ 10 \\ 10 \\ 15 \end{bmatrix} \]

Exact solution: \( x = [5, 5, 5, 5]^T \)

Step 1 — Check Diagonal Dominance

Row 1: \( |4| > |-1| = 1 \) ✓    Row 2: \( |4| > |-1|+|-1| = 2 \) ✓

Row 3: same as Row 2 ✓    Row 4: same as Row 1 ✓

All rows satisfy strict diagonal dominance → convergence guaranteed.

\[ x_1^{(k+1)} = \frac{15 + x_2^{(k)}}{4}, \quad x_2^{(k+1)} = \frac{10 + x_1^{(k)} + x_3^{(k)}}{4} \] \[ x_3^{(k+1)} = \frac{10 + x_2^{(k)} + x_4^{(k)}}{4}, \quad x_4^{(k+1)} = \frac{15 + x_3^{(k)}}{4} \]
